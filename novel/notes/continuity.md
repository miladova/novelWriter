# Continuity — A House of Graces

Working notes. Not part of the novel. Current through ch25 (draft complete).

## Calendar

- **Present action:** December 111 (ch1) to December 112 (ch25), i.e. Trajan's
  fifth and sixth consulships. The trial is in high summer 112; the Palatine
  house is sold 30 November 112; the Novus annus opens ch25, Saturnalia
  a.d. XVI Kal. Ian. (= 17 December) 112.
- **Flashback:** summer–autumn 88, last years of Domitian. Cassia is 18,
  Corvinus 20, at the villa at Tibur.
- **Sequence of ch13–25:** ch13 spring 112 → ch14 games, June → ch15 wedding,
  June → ch16–18 late June, the hearing and the arrests → ch19 July, the
  palace → ch20 deathbed, July → ch21–22 trial, high summer → ch23 August–
  November → ch24 12 December (a.d. pridie Id. Dec.) → ch25 17 December.
- Day counting is inclusive Roman style: a.d. XVI Kal. Ian. = 17 December.

## Houses and places

- **The House of the Graces**, domus on the Esquiline off the Clivus Suburanus,
  near the Porticus Liviae: green door, atrium, peristyle garden, three dining
  rooms (painted triclinium — damaged by the fire and under repair all year —
  garden stibadium, small winter room), bath suite, strongroom, service yard,
  back lane to a bakery. Fig tree planted in the garden c. 106. Sealed room at
  the top of the stairs, kept empty 24 years; occupied by Gaius at Saturnalia
  112. Shelf above the hearth: the six cups and the signed cup, never used.
- **The Sulpicius domus on the Palatine**, near the Scalae Caci: masks in the
  atrium, the three Graces in the alcove. Sold 30 November 112 to a brick
  magnate, to be demolished for an insula. The family moves to a smaller house
  on the Esquiline, two streets from Cassia; masks rehung by Aemilia.
- **Villa at Tibur:** sold after the father's death; now a wine merchant's.
  The cell at the back, the storeroom, the whitewashed wall with a child's
  scratched fish, the vineyard, the garden with poppies, hellebore, monkshood.
- **The potter's shop**, Via Ostiensis outside the Porta Trigemina: fig tree,
  yard, kiln, racks, wheel; a boy of twelve turns the wheel. The potter's mark
  is a bird pressed into the base.
- **The farm near Aricia** on Sittius's estate: where the boy was held, Aug 112.
- **The Curia:** trial venue (not the basilica). Closed session for the sealed
  testimony.
- **Ostia:** harbour, the Ship of Isis, the grain fleet; Philumena sent there.

## Names and titles

- **Emperor:** Imperator Caesar Nerva Trajanus Augustus Germanicus Dacicus.
  Dying of dropsy through ch19–25; leaves for the East autumn 113; dies at
  Selinus, summer 117.
- **Empress:** Pompeia Plotina. The bargain of ch19. Sends one letter at
  Saturnalia 112 via her steward.
- **Consuls 112:** Trajan VI and Titus Sextius Africanus.
- **Corvinus:** patrician; augur (stripped September 112); suffect consul 105;
  legionary legate in the Second Dacian War; curator of public works; candidate
  for Asia (struck from the candidacy by Paetus's motion, ch22); acquitted of
  parricide but **censured**.
- **Pactus:** plebeian, novus homo, consular. Convicted of calumny,
  subornation and arson; exiled, property confiscated, struck from the Senate.
- **Crispus:** prosecutor at the trial. **Sura:** young defence advocate.
  **Paetus:** moved the motion striking Corvinus from the Asian candidacy.
- **The emperor's secretary:** thin, grey, ex-treasury slave; wrote the Primus
  grain letter in 88; confesses in open session ch22.
- Cassia's full name: **Antonia Cassia** — Antonia from the house that freed
  her, Cassia her own.

## The buried past

- **88, Tibur.** Cassia, slave of the Sulpicii, 18, pregnant by Corvinus, 20.
  Corvinus's father found out on 21 September. Plan: sell the girl, dispose of
  the child, ship the son to Syria.
- **Sulpicia Varia's plan**, formed since spring 88: poison her husband slowly
  — a little in his wine at dinner, more in the posset, a third dose in the
  morning — using a debt-ridden physician, a debt-ridden steward and a
  frightened cook. No monkshood: something slower and easier to explain, on a
  man with a bad heart. Called a seizure.
- **The birth**, night in the last week of September 88, in the bolted cell.
  Cassia given poppy, honey, wine and three drops of bitter almond — mercy, so
  she would not remember or fight. She delivered a living son; the midwife
  Psyche saw him breathe; Varia had him taken away and told Cassia he was
  stillborn.
- **The sale:** Gaius Vibidius, potter, Via Ostiensis, paid 400 sesterces;
  chosen after three weeks' search *because he was kind* — a good trade, no
  children, a wife who could not conceive. Cassia was on a cart to Puteoli
  three days later, sold to the green door (brothel keeper).
- **Psyche's receipt** (the 400-sesterces chit) kept under her bed 24 years;
  she was paid a little every year to keep silent, refused to leave Tibur.
  Varia left her 100,000 sesterces in the will.
- **Cassia's mother: Daphne** — laundress at Tibur, sang at her work, died of a
  fever in the winter of Varia's 35th year (four days); called for Cassia for
  two days and Varia sat with her.
- **The boy:** Gaius Vibidius Nestor, 24, potter, born 28 September 88; has a
  girl in Ostia; foster mother died when he was seven (fever, three days),
  making the potter promise never to tell him he was bought.
- **The dealer:** Caepio. **The forger:** Secundus (statement ch21).

## The trial (chs 21–22)

- **Day one (a.d. VI Kal. Aug.):** charge read; Crispus for the prosecution; Sura for the defence;
  Aemilia and young Sulpicia in the gallery. Corvinus speaks for himself and
  admits his cowardice in 88. Closed session: Psyche testifies seated, the
  receipt is read, the names **Gaius Vibidius Nestor** and the potter struck
  from the record and sealed. Faustus (old steward, Ficulea), Secundus's dying
  statement, Cassia testifies ("paid vs trusted"). Cassia calls Anthus out of
  the back of the chamber: "I paid him." The emperor's secretary rises: Pactus
  to be examined tomorrow and is not to leave the city.
- **Day two:** Marcia (the woman from the prefect's courtyard) brings bread at
  dawn; Pactus prosecutes for two hours and accuses Cassia of being the
  palace's creature; her Livia joke. Anthus: the boy is held at the Aricia farm
  on Pactus's order. Cassia produces the Primus grain letter; the secretary
  admits it is his.
- **Verdicts:** Corvinus acquitted of parricide, censured, struck from the
  Asian candidacy (motion by Paetus). Pactus exiled, property confiscated,
  struck from the Senate. Sittius condemned in absentia — found dead at Baiae
  with opened wrists. Primus pensioned to Campania, no further action (dies
  November). Verecundus to the mines. Rufus to the games. Anthus to exile after
  confessing. Cassia's household cleared, costs remitted.
- **The seal:** the boy's name and the potter's name struck from the record and
  sealed by order of the house — Cassia's condition.
- **After the verdict:** the emperor's secretary stops Cassia in the portico:
  the seal will hold as long as the men who ordered it live; he is going to a
  house and a pension and a garden in Campania; "the one thing Rome cannot
  forgive is a person who does not want what it is selling."

## The will and the codicil (ch20)

- House and estate to Corvinus, with life interest to Aemilia.
- All household slaves freed.
- Jewellery chest to Cassia, "to be given to her unopened"; to be sold, the
  money given to the boy, told it is from his grandmother.
- 100,000 sesterces to Psyche.
- Codicil: *"I am asking you to be better than me."*
- Last words: "The masks. Polish the masks." Death at dawn, family present;
  the largest funeral on the Palatine that year; Corvinus's honest eulogy;
  Pactus at the tomb; Aemilia confronts Cassia about the jewellery and asks her
  to stand by young Sulpicia.

## Aftermath and ending (chs 23–25)

- Clients drop by a third; the augurate lost in September; Asian command to
  Verus. Sittius's heirs call the mortgage; house sold; move to the Esquiline.
- Ione returns in October, is rehired as bookkeeper and given the sums.
- Cassia tells the potter the truth about the sealed record (November); sends
  a three-line letter signed "A friend", withholding her name deliberately.
- **December:** six cups and a signed cup arrive ("For the house. From
  Gaius."); Cassia answers with a four-line letter signed "a friend" — no name.
  The name is spoken in person on 12 December (ch24).
- **The dresser:** Varia's dresser, thirty years in the Palatine house with a
  son of fourteen, meets Cassia at the tomb before the will is read; comes to
  the Esquiline in September and stays eleven years.
- **ch24, 12 December:** Cassia, Corvinus and Bathyllus at the shop. Cassia
  tells him: born 28 September 88, sold for 400 sesterces. The foster mother's
  death (aged seven). "Why did you give me up?" — "I did not give you up. I was
  robbed of you." He is angry and says so; he goes back to the wheel. He asks
  what to call her: "You may call me Cassia." The potter's fear that the boy
  will prefer the rich house; Cassia's answer that the craft is what cannot be
  taken away. Corvinus and Cassia at the gate: "I am not going to Baiae."
- **Saturnalia, 17 December 112:** whole household turned upside down;
  sigillum by Celer; thirty at three tables in the garden room (the painted
  triclinium still under repair). Guests: Tertulla, Aemilia, Corvinus, young
  Sulpicia, Aulus, Publius and **Calpurnia** (married since 1 November; he paid
  off the sailor with a loan against Varia's jewellery). Plotina's letter:
  manumissions commended; Primus dead in Campania in November; she has read the
  trial record. Cassia has sold the jewellery; the money is for the boy.
  **Nine manumission papers** read out: Sabina, Chrysis, Senno, Celer and five
  others; Thais, Hermeros and Bathyllus already free. Gaius arrives at the end
  of the evening with a lamp; meets the household; young Sulpicia says "I am
  your sister." Cassia and Corvinus in the garden. Next morning Gaius proposes
  a business partnership: "We could start there."
- **The letter chronology:** cups arrive end of November (a.d. IV Kal. Dec.);
  Cassia's four-line reply signed "a friend"; the potter refuses for two weeks;
  the meeting 12 December.
- **Coda:** Cassia lives another twenty-two years — the emperor leaves for the
  East in autumn 113 and dies at Selinus in 117; the boy marries his Ostian
  girl and has three children; Sabina goes into business with a ship's captain;
  Ione buys a lodging house; Aemilia and Corvinus grow old together; Cassia
  never marries, goes to Baiae once (114), keeps the house and the accounts to
  the end.

## Motifs and props

- **Doors and thresholds:** the green door; the bolted door of the cell; the
  door that locks before the truth is told.
- **Lamps and light:** the lamp Gaius makes for Saturnalia; lamps lit for the
  feast; the lamp in the cell.
- **Accounts:** the daybook, the black book (burned ch16), the receipt, the
  ledger, Ione's sums. "Paid vs trusted."
- **Names given and taken:** Gaius Vibidius Nestor; Antonia Cassia; "A friend";
  "You may call me Cassia."
- **Cups and clay:** the cup of 88; the six cups; the signed cup on the shelf;
  the potter's bird mark; the rough clay pigeon left at Nolus's grave.
- **Masks:** the Sulpicii masks, polished, moved twice, rehung by Aemilia.
- **The three Graces:** the alcove at the House of the Graces; the name of the
  house.
- **The fig tree:** planted for no reason; stands in both gardens; Corvinus
  notices it at Tibur (ch12).
- **Pigeons:** Nolus's eleven; the clay pigeon.
- **"The only mercy I had left to give."**
- **"I did not give you up. I was robbed of you."**
- **"That is what a mother is… standing in a room waiting for someone to come
  through the door."**
- **"We could start there."**
- **"A woman had built a house, and the house had held, and the people in it
  had lived."** (last line)

## Timeline of Nolus and the fire

- Nolus, 11, from the market two years before; eleven pigeons.
- Fire in March 112: storeroom/passage, door bolted from outside; Nolus dies.
- Buried outside the Porta Esquilina; stone: A GOOD BOY, WHO FED THE PIGEONS.
  Annual household offering on the day of his death; Gaius joins from March
  113.

## Open threads / things to keep consistent in revision

  Philumena at Ostia and Ione's return in October.
- Ch23 says "at forty-two, sitting at the head of a table"; Cassia is 42 through
  the whole book, so this is fine, but keep her age fixed at 42 through ch25
  (her birthday falls outside the narrative).
- Publius/Calpurnia is resolved only in ch25's entrance; if expanded, the loan
  against Varia's jewellery must not touch Cassia's chest (sold separately).
- Ch25 says Sabina is thirteen "now"; she is twelve at the start of 112, so her
  birthday falls between January and December. Consistent as written.
